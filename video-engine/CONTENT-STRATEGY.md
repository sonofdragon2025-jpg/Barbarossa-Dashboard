# Short-form clipping content strategy — draft-only

## Niches
1. Nostalgic or visually engaging family animation (e.g., SpongeBob, Bluey, Disney/Pixar). Family Guy is adult animation; tag separately, never label it children's content.
2. High-energy digital influencers and podcasters (e.g., MrBeast, Kai Cenat, IShowSpeed, xQc).

Names are discovery candidates, **not** permissions to reuse footage. Use only licensed/owner-authorized source assets, or original commentary and original animation. Verify rights for any Minecraft/Subway Surfers gameplay and music as well.

## Discovery and source verification
- Identify source creator, official episode/video title, canonical link, and date where possible.
- Obtain a playable, authorized source and record its license or explicit permission.
- Inspect the actual source before suggesting timestamps. Never fabricate an exact timestamp; use "not verified" until reviewed.
- Record rights provenance, asset ownership, monetization restrictions, audience suitability, and platform policy considerations.
- Avoid deceptive edits, manufactured reactions, false quotes, or misrepresenting context.

## Clip construction
- Duration: 30–60 seconds.
- 0–3 s: authentic high-stakes question, immediate reaction, or visual hook.
- 3–45 s: brisk, truthful progression, remove filler while preserving context.
- 45–60 s: genuine payoff or accurate cliffhanger; favor natural loopability.
- Prefer 9:16 at 1080x1920, with safe-zone caption placement away from platform overlays.
- Captions: speech-aligned, bold high-contrast kinetic text; selectively highlight emotional words in yellow/green. Use properly licensed fonts.
- Optional split screen: top = primary authorized footage; bottom = separately licensed/owned gameplay or satisfying visuals. Don't add split-screen just to evade copyright or platform reuse rules.
- Sound: use licensed tracks/SFX; -15 dB is an initial background-music target, not a guarantee of intelligibility. Check final dialogue balance and loudness.
- Add zooms, punch-ins and SFX sparingly and only where they accurately emphasize the source.
- Quality review: source permissions, accuracy, caption synchronization, legibility, speech audibility, flashing/age suitability, and platform eligibility.
